I want to rebuild a public dataset from its original sources, without
any starting files. Please do the following and show your work — and
know one thing going in: part of this source moves every day, so your
copy will be today's page, not the one this book captured.

THE FIRST SOURCE. Wikipedia's page "2026 United States House of
Representatives elections", downloaded as raw wikitext through the
MediaWiki API:
https://en.wikipedia.org/w/api.php?action=parse&page=2026_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections&prop=wikitext&format=json&formatversion=2
No account or key is needed. Two tables inside it matter: the
comparison of generic-ballot polling aggregators, and the summary of
mid-decade redistricting. Wikipedia is not the authority on either —
it is merely the one place six aggregators who publish no common file
sit in a single table.

THE SECOND SOURCE. The @unitedstates project's roster of sitting
members of Congress:
https://unitedstates.github.io/congress-legislators/legislators-current.csv
One CSV; each row is a current senator or representative, with party,
state and Senate class. No key.

WHAT TO BUILD.
1. The aggregators' national House margins, read out of the wikitext
   table, with their average.
2. From the roster: the Senate seats up in November 2026 — class 2,
   plus any special elections — with each seat's current party.
3. A sanity baseline: the 2024 House result the forecast starts from,
   215 Democratic seats to 220 Republican, with every district given a
   starting vote share.

CHECK YOUR WORK. The copy this book used was captured on 16 August
2026. The aggregator table will have moved since — that drift is the
page being edited, not an error. What should still hold:
- the baseline reproduces the 2024 House: 215 Democratic seats
- all 435 districts carry a starting share
- 537 rows in the roster file, and its senators sum to exactly 100
The roster updates whenever a seat changes hands mid-term; a count a
few rows off means a vacancy or a swearing-in, not a mistake.
