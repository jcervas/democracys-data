The core file behind this chapter could not be downloaded when this book's
copy was built, and the story of that failure is worth having before
you try.

WHY NOT. The chapter is built on U.S. Census Bureau, "Frequently
Occurring Surnames from the 2010 Census" — every surname held by 100 or
more people, with the share of its bearers in each of six race and
ethnicity categories. In August 2026 its address,
https://www2.census.gov/topics/genealogy/2010surnames/names.zip
answered as if everything were fine, but what came back was a 247-byte
page reading "Request Rejected" instead of the zip — while everything
else in the same directory, and the whole 2020 release, arrived
normally. A server that refuses one file and serves its neighbors may
change its mind without notice, in either direction. So try the address
first: if a real zip arrives (about 13 MB, holding the table as a CSV
and a spreadsheet), you hold the genuine article, and the numbers below
will tell you so. The mind-change has in fact happened once already: on
16 August 2026 the same address served the real zip, and that capture —
the Bureau's own artifact, not a local re-assembly — ships with this
chapter's data as names.zip, beside the CSV extracted from it. If the
address refuses you, nothing is lost.

WHAT EXISTS INSTEAD. The matching 2020 release downloads normally:
"Frequently Occurring Surnames in the 2020 Census", spreadsheets at
https://www2.census.gov/topics/genealogy/2020surnames/
(the last-name files are the ones named LastNames). Most of what this
chapter does can be repeated on it. The differences you would see are
real differences between the two censuses, plus a change in how much
the Bureau suppressed.

WHAT YOU CAN STILL CHECK. However you obtain the 2010 file — the
address relenting, the copy shipped with this chapter, an archive, a
colleague — these numbers say whether you are holding the same object:
- 162,254 data rows, of which the last is a residual called
  ALL OTHER NAMES rather than a name
- eleven columns: name, rank, count, two proportion columns, and six
  percentage columns for the race categories, with suppressed cells
  printed as (S)
- SMITH first, at 2,442,977 bearers
- the zip itself: 12,874,389 bytes, unpacking to that CSV and an .xlsx
  of the same table
