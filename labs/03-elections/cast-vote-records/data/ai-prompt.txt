I want to rebuild a public dataset from its original source, without
any starting files. Please do the following and show your work.

THE SOURCE. State of Alaska, Division of Elections, cast vote record
export for the 2024 general election:
https://www.elections.alaska.gov/results/24GENR/CVR_Export_20241130154411.zip
No account or key is needed. The zip is about 44 MB and expands to
about 1.58 GB of JSON. Inside, one "session" per scanned ballot, each
carrying cards, contests, and marks; every mark names a candidate and
a rank. The U.S. Representative contest carries the Id 7, and a
manifest file inside the zip maps candidate Ids to names. This is the
closest thing to a public record of individual votes that exists in
the United States. The state's own round-by-round result for the same
race is a small PDF beside it, RCV-USRep.pdf, on the same site. Two
warnings from Alaska itself: ballots counted by hand are not in the
export, and write-in names are not shown, so the file will not
reproduce the certified totals exactly -- and is not supposed to.

HOW TO DOWNLOAD IT. The download itself is plain. The size is the difficulty:
read the JSON files one at a time from inside the zip rather than
expanding all of it into memory at once.

WHAT TO BUILD.
1. Every ballot's ranking in the U.S. Representative race, collapsed
   to distinct ranking sequences with a count of how many ballots
   cast each. Nothing identifying any voter survives the collapse.
2. Your own instant-runoff count from those sequences, eliminating
   the last-place candidate each round, next to the state's official
   round-by-round result.

CHECK YOUR WORK. The copy this book used was downloaded in August 2026.
If your rebuild matches it, you should find:
- 2,043 JSON files of ballots inside the archive
- 340,778 ballots rank someone in this race, collapsing to 926
  distinct ranking sequences
- the official final round: Begich 164,861 votes to Peltola's
  156,985 -- 51.22 against 48.78 percent
- your own count reproduces those percentages exactly, while the raw
  vote counts sit about 150 ballots apart in 322,000; the official
  tabulator applies exhaustion rules the simple count does not
This is an archived election, so the file should not change; the
timestamp in its name would change if the state ever reposted it.
