Part of the data behind this chapter comes from a source no assistant
can download for you -- though you can download it yourself, once, in a
browser.

WHY NOT. Georgia's Secretary of State publishes one archive per
election, covering all 159 counties down to the precinct, at
https://sos.ga.gov/page/historical-elections-results
The address is stable and public and a browser downloads it fine,
but the site sits behind bot protection: a script is turned away
even with browser headers. The November 2020 general election and
its recount are two separate archives there, and each is one manual
browser download. Everything after that download is automatic. The
finest-grained public record of American voting is served from
behind a bot check, and that fact is part of the subject.

WHAT EXISTS INSTEAD. Download the two 2020 archives yourself from
the page above. The 2024 general election needs no browser at all:
the state's JSON export,
https://results.sos.ga.gov/cdn/results/Georgia/export-2024NovGen.json
(about 34 MB), downloads cleanly by script. One more fact about what
the state published: the recount archive carries county summaries
for all 159 counties but precinct detail for exactly one county,
Lanier -- so the original count and the recount can only be compared
county by county.

WHAT YOU CAN STILL CHECK. These numbers say whether you are holding
the same objects:
- the 2020 archive yields 2,656 precincts across 159 counties
- the recount reproduces Georgia's certified 2020 totals exactly --
  Biden 2,473,633, Trump 2,461,854 -- while the original precinct
  archive gives Biden 2,474,507 and Trump 2,461,837: the same
  election counted twice, 874 Biden votes apart
- the 2024 export sums to Trump 2,663,117 and Harris 2,548,017,
  matching the certified statewide figures
