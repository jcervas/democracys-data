Two files behind this chapter. One refetches on its own; the other comes
from a source no assistant can download for you -- though you can
download it yourself, once, in a browser, at no charge and with no login.

WHY NOT. The 1976-2024 returns are the MIT Election Data and Science
Lab's "U.S. House 1976-2024," Harvard Dataverse, V15:
https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/IG0UN2. It is openly licensed (CC0) and
free. But Dataverse gates this particular file behind a guestbook -- a
name/email/purpose form a browser must submit before the download
starts. A scripted request is not throttled or blocked quietly; it
comes back immediately with HTTP 400 and the body
{"status":"ERROR","message":"You may not download this file without
the required Guestbook response for guestbookID 458."} The obstacle is
a form, not a technical wall, but no script can fill out a form on a
publisher's website on a stranger's behalf.

WHAT EXISTS INSTEAD, for that half. Open the DOI above in a browser,
fill in the guestbook once, and download two files:
1976-2024-house.tab (the returns) and codebook-us-house-1976-2024.md
(the variable list).

THE OTHER HALF FETCHES CLEANLY. Gary Jacobson's own replication data
for "It's Nothing Personal: The Decline of the Incumbency Advantage in
U.S. House Elections" (Journal of Politics, 2015) sits at
https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/29559, no guestbook, and this chapter's
build script (build-jacobson-series.R) downloads JOPrepfile1.tab
directly from
https://dataverse.harvard.edu/api/access/datafile/:persistentId?persistentId=doi:10.7910/DVN/29559/19HFER

WHAT YOU CAN STILL CHECK. If you obtain both files, these numbers say
whether you are holding the same objects:
- JOPrepfile1.tab is exactly 1,552,612 bytes: 30,005 observations,
  15,228 of them real (the rest is padding with no year at all),
  1946-2014
- 1976-2024-house.tab is exactly 4,156,562 bytes: 33,805 observations,
  1 row per candidate per party line per district-year
- restricted to general elections, the 50 states, non-special and
  non-write-in rows, 30,853 rows remain
- collapsed to one row per district-year, the file yields 10,861
  district-year races from 1976 through 2024
- of those, 9,425 are standard, contested, two-party races; 1,393 have
  no major-party opponent at all; 110 are top-two or jungle-primary
  ballots that put two candidates from the same party in the November
  race, where a two-party share has no meaning
- Minnesota's Republican nominees are recorded as "Independent-
  Republican" in 1992 (that was the party's legal name in the state
  from 1975 to 1995) -- 8 rows, all 8 the only Republican candidate in
  their race
- four district-years went to a runoff and are stored as two rounds
  under the same candidates: Texas's 8th, 9th and 25th in 1996, and
  Louisiana's 5th in 2002
- over the 16 years both files cover, the two files' incumbency codings
  agree on 4,440 of 4,895 compared races (90.7%)
