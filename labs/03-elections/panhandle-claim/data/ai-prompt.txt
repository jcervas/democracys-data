Most of this chapter's data can be fetched again today. One tier
cannot, and the reason it cannot is one of the chapter's own points.

WHAT STILL FETCHES. Everything the argument actually rests on.
Florida's Department of State publishes county-by-county results for
the 18 August 2026 primary, every office, as an open download:
https://flelectionfiles.floridados.gov/enightfilespublic/20260818_ElecResultsFL.txt
The 2018 and 2014 primaries come from the same Division's results
archive at https://results.elections.myflorida.com/ -- pick the
election, then the download utility. And the book-closing reports at
https://dos.fl.gov/elections/data-statistics/voter-registration-statistics/bookclosing/
give active registered voters by county, party and race for this
election, which is the file that closes the argument.

WHY THE PRECINCT TIER DOES NOT. Florida compiles a statewide precinct
file, and it was not out when this was written: the 2024 primary's
appeared ten months after that election. So precinct results existed
only where each of 67 county supervisors put them, at addresses
holding a per-county election id and a per-file UUID that nothing
indexes. Those ids were found by scanning and are recorded in
raw/enr_county_manifest.tsv. Counties retire the pages. Note that the
chapter uses those returns to show what the standard method would and
would not add, so a rebuild that skips them loses an example rather
than a result. Florida's voter file, which carries race, party and
precinct on every record, is public and free but needs a written
request to the Division of Elections; the chapter explains why having
it would not change the answer.

WHAT YOU CAN STILL CHECK. These numbers say whether you are holding
the same objects:
- Byron Donalds won the Republican primary for governor with 47.79%
  of an 11-candidate field and 1697262 votes, ranging from 18.02% in
  Lafayette County to 65.84% in Collier
- the other three statewide Republican winners on that ballot spread
  much less across the counties -- 20.6, 25.6 and 16.0 points against
  the governor's 47.8
- Ron DeSantis, white, won the 2018 primary for the same office with
  56.49% and a WIDER county spread, 51.9 points, and the two maps
  correlate at 0.72
- in the Panhandle, 7544 of 525542 registered Republicans are Black,
  which is 1.4%
- across all 67 counties the Black share of registered Republicans
  runs from 0.4% to 4.7%, while the Hispanic share runs from 0.7% to
  75.8%
