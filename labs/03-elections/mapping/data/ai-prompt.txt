I want to rebuild a public dataset from its original sources,
without any starting files. Please do the following and show your
work.

THE FIRST SOURCE. U.S. Census Bureau, cartographic boundary file
for counties, 1:5,000,000 scale, 2024 vintage:
https://www2.census.gov/geo/tiger/GENZ2024/shp/cb_2024_us_county_5m.zip
A zip holding one shapefile; each feature is a county or
county-equivalent, with its code, name, and land area. No account
or key is needed.

THE SECOND SOURCE. The Bureau's 2020 county population centroids --
the mean center of population for every county -- a plain text file:
https://www2.census.gov/geo/docs/reference/cenpop2020/county/CenPop2020_Mean_CO.txt

THE THIRD SOURCE. County-level 2024 presidential returns from the
tonmcg compilation on GitHub
(tonmcg/US_County_Level_Election_Results_08-24), the 2024 CSV
fetched raw from the master branch: one row per county with
Democratic, Republican, and total votes.

WHAT TO BUILD.
1. A map frame: every county that can be drawn and joined to a
   return -- code, name, state, votes, winner, land area, and
   density -- dropping territories and units the returns file does
   not carry.
2. The land-against-people arithmetic that maps of this election
   encode: the share of counties and of land area carried by each
   party, next to the share of actual votes.

CHECK YOUR WORK. The copies this book used were fetched in August
2026. If your rebuild matches them, you should find:
- the boundary zip is 2,982,952 bytes and holds 3,235 features;
  after the drops, 3,109 counties are mapped and 126 features are
  set aside
- the returns file has 3,160 rows carrying 154,863,739 votes
- the centroid file has 3,221 rows
- the Republican candidate carries 2,661 of the 3,109 counties --
  6,560,363 of 7,655,495 square kilometers of land -- while winning
  about half the vote; that gap is the point
The Bureau reissues the boundary and centroid files by vintage, and
the compilation can change without notice; a difference means a
source moved, not that you made an error.
