THE SOURCE. The Supreme Court Database, maintained at Washington
University in St. Louis — every case the Court has decided since 1946,
coded by researchers from the written opinions, because the Court
itself publishes opinions and no data. Release 2024_01, the
justice-centered file organised by citation:
http://scdb.wustl.edu/_brickFiles/2024_01/SCDB_2024_01_justiceCentered_Citation.csv.zip
About 1.7 MB zipped. Each row is one justice's vote in one case — more
than 80,000 rows. Two coded columns matter most: direction
(1 conservative, 2 liberal, blank where the coders could not say) and
majority (1 dissent, 2 majority).

HOW TO DOWNLOAD IT. The site serves over plain http only; an https
address fails outright. That is the source, not a problem with your
tooling — say so and proceed. The file is UTF-8; read it as such, or
the curly apostrophes in case names silently corrupt.

WHAT TO BUILD.
1. Per term, 1946 through 2023: how many cases were decided and what
   share went in the conservative direction.
2. For the 2020 through 2023 terms: every pair of justices and how
   often the two voted the same direction, with the number of cases
   they shared — and, per justice, how often each was in the majority.
