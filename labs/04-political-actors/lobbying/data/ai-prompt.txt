I want to rebuild a public dataset from its original source, without any
starting files. Please do the following and show your work.

THE SOURCE. The Lobbying Disclosure Act database, run by the Senate
and the Clerk of the House. Every lobbying firm files a quarterly
report naming its client, its lobbyists, the issues worked and the
money. The filings API is free, no account or key:
https://lda.gov/api/v1/filings/?filing_year=2024&filing_period=second_quarter&filing_type=Q2
(The older address, lda.senate.gov, now redirects there.) Each record
is one quarterly filing; the second quarter of 2024 held about
nineteen thousand of them.

HOW TO FETCH IT. Two traps. First, the filter: leave filing_type=Q2
off and the API still answers, cheerfully, with registration records
whose money fields are null — a table of the right shape and almost
entirely zeros, and nothing warns you. Second, the pace: an anonymous
caller gets 25 records per request and about fifteen requests a
minute, so collecting sixty pages takes several patient minutes, and
the whole quarter would take hours nobody owes you.

WHAT TO BUILD.
1. From the first sixty pages, a table of 1,500 filings: client,
   registrant, client state, the dollar amount (a firm reports income,
   an organisation lobbying for itself reports expenses — fold the two
   together), how many issue areas, and how many distinct lobbyists.
2. A list of the distinct lobbyists named, keeping people apart by the
   database's own id rather than by name — namesakes are real people,
   not duplicates.
3. The denominator: the total count of these filings the API reports
   for the quarter, so the sample's size is stated against it.

CHECK YOUR WORK. The copy this book used was fetched in August 2026.
If your rebuild matches it, you should find:
- 1,500 filings collected — 7.9% of the 19,016 the quarter held that
  day
- 773 distinct lobbyists across them
- without filing_type=Q2, the same query claimed 23,989 records
The quarter's count is live — filings are amended for years — and the
API's default ordering decides which 1,500 you get, so expect the
sample to move. Drift here is the database breathing, not an error.
