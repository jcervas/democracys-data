Sources and method

Ages come from Voteview's HSall_members.csv, one row per member per Congress since 1789, presidents included. Voteview records a birth year, not a date, so an age here is the age a member turns during that calendar year: right for anyone whose birthday has passed, a year high for everyone else, and about half a year high on average across a chamber. That is precise enough for medians and trends and too coarse for any claim about a particular person on a particular day.

Each Congress is spread across both calendar years it occupies, so a year shows that Congress's full roster — a seat that changed hands mid-term shows both holders in both years. A president who left in March belongs to the previous Congress: 1841 seated three presidents, but Van Buren sits with the 26th Congress and only Harrison and Tyler appear here under 1841.

Birth years are missing for __NOBORN__ of __NLEG__ legislators, who are left out of the medians and counted beside them. Four presidents — Washington, John Adams, Taylor and Hoover — have no birth year on any Voteview row, and a fifth, Cleveland, carries one on his first-term rows but not his second. Cleveland's own value is filled in from those rows; the other four use uncontested dates from the standard presidential record and are marked † wherever they appear. Every other file in this lab keeps Voteview's blanks untouched, so its validation output still reports the gap.

Washington and Adams are also why a birth year is slipperier than it looks: both were born under the Julian calendar, and Washington's 22 February 1732 New Style was 11 February 1731/2 Old Style — a different year, from the same birth.

Territorial delegates appear only from the 113th Congress, which is how Voteview carries them, so House counts sit at about 435 throughout.
