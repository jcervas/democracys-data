I want to rebuild a public dataset from its original sources, without any
starting files. Please do the following and show your work.

THE SOURCES.
1. Adam Bonica's Database on Ideology, Money in Politics, and Elections
   (DIME), public version 4.0, from https://data.stanford.edu/dime. Download
   the "Recipients/Candidates (1979-2024)" file in .csv.gz form, about 30 MB.
   Each row is one candidate in one election cycle. Keep rows where seat is
   "federal:house", recipient.type is "cand" and party is 100 (Democrat) or
   200 (Republican). The score to use is recipient.cfscore.
2. The MIT Election Data and Science Lab's "U.S. House 1976-2024" returns,
   https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/IG0UN2 (download through a browser; the
   repository does not serve scripts). Keep general elections that are not
   specials or runoffs, from 1980 on.

WHAT TO BUILD.
1. One row per major-party House nominee: the candidate with the most votes
   for each party in each district, summing a candidate's votes across all
   the ballot lines they appeared on. Count Minnesota's DFL and North
   Dakota's Democratic-NPL as Democrats.
2. Match each nominee to a DIME record by cycle, state, party, district and
   last name. Drop any match whose num.givers.total is below 10.
3. Within each cycle, rescale the scores so the average Democratic nominee
   is 0 and the average Republican nominee is 1.
4. For every race where both nominees have a score, subtract the
   Democrat's position from the Republican's. Count the races where the
   result is zero or negative.

CHECK YOUR WORK. The copy this book used was downloaded in October 2026.
If your rebuild matches it, you should find:
- 18,688 major-party nominees from 1980 to 2024
- 16,471 of them with a usable score
- 6,514 races with both nominees scored
- in 2024, 354 races with both nominees scored
Small differences are expected, because last-name matching can be done
more than one way. A large difference in the 2024 count probably means
DIME has been updated since.
